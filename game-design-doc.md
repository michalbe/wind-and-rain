# WIND & RAIN
## First Playable Game Design Document — v0.1

### 1. Game Overview

**Working title:** Wind & Rain

**Genre:** 3D real-time strategy

**Visual target:** Early-2000s PC RTS aesthetic. Extremely low-poly models, hand-painted-looking textures, exaggerated silhouettes, simple animation, readable units viewed from an elevated RTS camera.

**Setting:** Mythological early-medieval Slavic Central Europe.

**Core fantasy:** The player leads a small Slavic settlement whose economy is powered by ritual. Priestesses dance to gather Wind, priests perform rites to gather Rain, and those two resources are used to expand the village and raise a small army.

The first playable should feel immediately understandable to someone familiar with Warcraft III or StarCraft while having a distinct visual and mechanical identity.

---

# 2. First Playable Goal

The first playable is not intended to demonstrate the complete game.

Its purpose is to prove that these elements are fun together:

- RTS camera and controls
- selecting units
- constructing a village
- ritual-based resource gathering
- training military units
- expanding toward a Rain resource
- fighting a small enemy force
- destroying an enemy settlement

Target session length:

**10–20 minutes**

The player should finish the prototype thinking:

> “I understand this game, and I want to see what the supernatural parts become.”

---

# 3. Core Gameplay Loop

The basic loop is:

**Gather Wind → Gather Rain → Build → Train → Explore → Fight → Expand**

Wind is the common resource.

Rain is the scarce strategic resource.

The player begins with a small settlement and several ritual units.

Vietras generate Wind.

Zhercas collect Rain.

The player spends those resources on buildings and military units.

Military units protect the settlement, clear hostile areas, and eventually attack the enemy village.

---

# 4. Resources

## WIND 🌬

Wind is the primary resource.

It represents energy, spirit, movement, and human ritual power.

Wind is generated by **Vietras**.

A Vietra performs a ritual dance near the Grod or a designated ritual area.

While dancing, she generates Wind continuously.

For the first playable:

**1 Vietra = 1 Wind per second**

The number is deliberately simple and can later be rebalanced.

Vietras stop producing Wind when:

- ordered to move
- constructing a building
- killed
- interrupted by combat

This makes economic harassment immediately understandable.

---

## RAIN 🌧

Rain is the advanced resource.

It represents fertility, life, magic, and supernatural power.

Rain is gathered by **Zhercas**.

Rain comes from fixed locations on the map called **Sacred Springs**.

The player builds a **Rain Shrine** over or beside a Sacred Spring.

A Zherca assigned to the shrine performs a ritual and generates Rain.

For the prototype:

**1 Zherca = 1 Rain per second**

Rain locations are intentionally limited.

This forces players to leave the safety of their starting settlement.

---

# 5. Starting State

The player begins with:

**1 Grod**

**3 Vietras**

**1 Zherca**

Starting resources:

**50 Wind**

**0 Rain**

The three Vietras begin the match dancing automatically.

A Sacred Spring is located near the starting settlement.

The opening state should visually explain the game's economy before the player reads anything.

---

# 6. Player Units

The first playable contains only six controllable unit types.

## VIETRA

**Role:** Wind Priestess / economic unit / builder

**Cost:** 50 Wind

**Supply:** 1

Primary abilities:

**Wind Dance**

Vietra stops moving and performs a ritual dance.

Generates Wind continuously.

**Build**

Vietra can construct basic buildings.

Combat ability should be extremely weak or nonexistent.

The Vietra should visually read as a ritual unit rather than a conventional worker.

---

## ZHERCA

**Role:** Rain Priest / advanced economic unit / ritualist

**Cost:** 50 Wind

**Supply:** 1

Primary abilities:

**Rain Rite**

When assigned to a Rain Shrine, the Zherca performs a ritual that gathers Rain.

**Build**

Zherca can construct Rain Shrines and Sacred Groves.

For the first playable, the Zherca does not need combat spells.

---

## STRELETZ

**Role:** Basic ranged infantry

**Cost:** 50 Wind

**Supply:** 1

Weapon:

Bow

Characteristics:

Cheap
Fast to produce
Low health
Good basic damage
Strong in groups

This is the player's easiest military unit.

---

## VITEZ

**Role:** Heavy melee warrior

**Cost:** 100 Wind + 25 Rain

**Supply:** 2

Weapon:

Large axe, sword, spear, or combination depending on final art direction.

Characteristics:

High health
Good melee damage
Slow compared with lighter units
Protects Streletz units

The Vitez should have a large, immediately recognizable silhouette.

---

## DEER

For the first playable, this is a **Deer Rider** rather than an ordinary deer.

**Role:** Fast scout / raider

**Cost:** 50 Wind + 50 Rain

**Supply:** 1

Characteristics:

Very fast
Low durability
Good vision
Useful for finding enemy locations and attacking exposed ritualists

The exaggerated visual should make the deer significantly larger than a realistic animal.

---

## BEAR

**Role:** Heavy beast

**Cost:** 100 Wind

**Supply:** 2

Characteristics:

Slow
High health
Strong melee attack
Effective against buildings

The Bear requires the Sacred Grove.

The Bear should feel slightly mysterious. The player does not need to know whether it is domesticated, magically bonded, or summoned during the first prototype.

---

# 7. Future Unit — Not Required for First Playable

## BABA

**Role:** Witch / seer / supernatural support unit

Possible cost:

**150 Wind + 125 Rain**

Possible abilities:

- curse
- reveal fog of war
- heal
- summon spirit
- frighten enemies

Baba should not be included unless the basic combat prototype is already working extremely well.

She belongs to the next development milestone.

---

# 8. Buildings

The first playable should contain a maximum of five functional building types.

## GROD

Primary settlement building.

Functions:

- trains Vietras
- trains Zhercas
- acts as the main Wind ritual location
- determines player defeat

Prototype rebuild cost:

**300 Wind**

The starting Grod is already constructed.

---

## KHATA

Population building.

Cost:

**100 Wind**

Provides:

**+8 Supply**

Small wooden house.

Several Khatas together should visually transform the starting area into a village.

---

## WAR HALL

Military production building.

Cost:

**150 Wind**

Produces:

- Streletz
- Vitez
- Deer Rider

The Bear is not produced here.

---

## RAIN SHRINE

Economic building.

Cost:

**75 Wind**

Must be constructed at a Sacred Spring.

Allows Zhercas to collect Rain.

Visually it could contain:

- wooden idol
- stone basin
- hanging cloth
- bowls
- carved poles
- water pool

---

## SACRED GROVE

Advanced building.

Cost:

**150 Wind + 50 Rain**

Unlocks:

- Bear

Later versions can use this building for Baba and supernatural upgrades.

Visually it should look less like conventional architecture and more like a ritual space:

stones, sacred trees, wooden idols, bones, ribbons, masks, or carved poles.

---

# 9. Population

The game uses a simple supply system.

The Grod provides starting supply.

Suggested:

**Starting cap: 10**

Each Khata adds:

**+8**

The first playable should have a maximum practical army size of approximately:

**20–30 units**

Large armies are deliberately outside prototype scope.

---

# 10. First Playable Map

The prototype requires only one map.

Working map name:

**The Sacred Valley**

Approximate structure:

Player settlement in southwest.

Nearby Sacred Spring.

Forest dividing the center of the map.

Small neutral threat in the center.

Second Sacred Spring in a strategically exposed location.

Enemy village in northeast.

The terrain should naturally guide the player without obvious artificial corridors.

Important features:

- hills
- pine forest
- small river or marsh
- open meadow
- Sacred Springs
- enemy village
- one mysterious supernatural landmark

The map should be small enough that crossing it with infantry takes approximately one minute.

---

# 11. Enemy

The first enemy faction should reuse the player's models wherever possible.

This saves enormous production time.

Enemy faction:

**Rival Clan**

Their units use different:

- clothing colors
- shields
- banners
- settlement markings

They can use the same:

- Vietra
- Zherca
- Streletz
- Vitez
- Deer
- Bear

No second faction design is required.

The rival clan AI only needs to perform basic behaviors:

- gather resources
- train soldiers
- defend its settlement
- occasionally send a small attack
- replace lost units

Complex strategic AI is unnecessary.

---

# 12. Neutral Creature

The map should contain exactly one supernatural creature type.

Suggested:

**Forest Spirit**

Working name can remain generic during development.

The creature protects a forest clearing in the center of the map.

Purpose:

- introduce supernatural atmosphere
- provide an optional fight
- demonstrate that the world contains more than human factions
- create anticipation for later mythology systems

The creature does not need complicated mechanics.

For the prototype it can simply be:

high health
slow
dangerous melee attack

Later it can evolve into a proper Leshy-style territory mechanic.

---

# 13. Player Objective

Primary objective:

**Destroy the rival Grod.**

Secondary tutorial objectives appear sequentially.

Example:

**Gather 100 Wind**

**Build a Khata**

**Build a War Hall**

**Train 3 Streletz**

**Build a Rain Shrine**

**Gather 50 Rain**

**Train a Vitez**

**Find the rival settlement**

**Destroy the rival Grod**

The objectives teach the game without a separate tutorial level.

---

# 14. Combat Model

Combat should initially be extremely simple.

No armor types.

No complex damage tables.

No hero system.

No active combat abilities.

Every unit needs only:

- Health
- Damage
- Attack speed
- Attack range
- Movement speed
- Supply cost

Prototype combat relationships:

**Streletz**

Good ranged damage but fragile.

**Vitez**

Durable frontline melee unit.

**Deer Rider**

Fast and good at attacking exposed economic units.

**Bear**

Slow, durable, strong against structures.

This creates enough tactical variation for the first playable.

---

# 15. Controls

Controls should follow classic RTS conventions.

Left click:

Select unit.

Drag:

Box selection.

Right click terrain:

Move.

Right click enemy:

Attack.

Right click resource structure:

Assign economic unit.

Control groups:

Ctrl + number

Camera:

Edge scrolling and WASD optional.

Mouse wheel:

Zoom.

Keyboard shortcuts should exist but are not essential for the earliest internal build.

---

# 16. Camera

The camera should resemble Warcraft III more than a modern strategy game.

Characteristics:

Moderately close to units.

Perspective rather than perfectly top-down.

Units should visibly have faces, weapons, and silhouettes.

Player can zoom slightly but cannot rotate freely during the first prototype.

Limiting camera movement simplifies environment production.

---

# 17. Visual Direction

The game should intentionally resemble a forgotten PC strategy game from approximately 2002–2004.

Not modern realism.

Not detailed modern low-poly art.

Targets:

Very low polygon counts.

Small texture sizes.

Chunky geometry.

Large weapons.

Large heads and hands.

Simple shadows.

Simple particles.

Strong unit silhouettes.

Limited animation frames.

Buildings should look handmade and uneven.

Wooden beams should be oversized.

Roof shapes should dominate building silhouettes.

Forest geometry should be simple enough that dozens of trees can appear without visual noise.

---

# 18. Animation

The most important animation in the entire prototype is:

**Vietra Wind Dance**

It should be readable from normal RTS camera distance.

Possible movement:

arms raised
slow spin
stamping feet
cloth movement
staff movement

Wind particles should circle the Vietra while she dances.

The second most important animation:

**Zherca Rain Rite**

Possible actions:

kneeling
raising a vessel
pouring water
striking the ground with a staff
chanting

These animations communicate the game's core concept.

Combat animation quality is secondary.

---

# 19. Audio

Audio should reinforce the ritual economy.

Wind Dance:

- soft vocal rhythm
- ankle bells
- wooden percussion
- wind sound

Rain Rite:

- low chanting
- water sounds
- drum
- distant thunder

When several Vietras are dancing simultaneously, their audio should combine into a subtle settlement rhythm.

The economy should therefore create part of the game's ambient soundtrack.

Combat should remain simple and punchy.

Axes, bows, shouting, animal sounds.

---

# 20. User Interface

The UI should be intentionally simple.

Top resource bar:

🌬 **WIND: 325**

🌧 **RAIN: 75**

**SUPPLY: 12 / 18**

Selected-unit panel shows:

Name
Portrait
Health
Commands

Example:

**VIETRA**

Wind Priestess

[Dance]

[Build]

Avoid large amounts of explanatory text during gameplay.

---

# 21. Prototype Economy

Initial temporary costs:

| Unit / Building | Wind | Rain | Supply |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Vietra | 50 | 0 | 1 |
| Zherca | 50 | 0 | 1 |
| Streletz | 50 | 0 | 1 |
| Vitez | 100 | 25 | 2 |
| Deer Rider | 50 | 50 | 1 |
| Bear | 100 | 0 | 2 |
| Khata | 100 | 0 | — |
| War Hall | 150 | 0 | — |
| Rain Shrine | 75 | 0 | — |
| Sacred Grove | 150 | 50 | — |
| Grod | 300 | 0 | — |

These numbers are prototype constants only.

They are intentionally borrowed from familiar RTS economic relationships so development can focus on whether the game loop works.

They should not be considered final balance.

---

# 22. Example Match Flow

### Minute 0

Player starts with Grod, three Vietras and one Zherca.

Vietras are dancing.

Player immediately begins accumulating Wind.

### Minute 1

Player trains another Vietra.

Builds a Khata.

### Minute 2

Player constructs War Hall.

### Minute 3

First Streletz units are trained.

Player begins scouting.

### Minute 4

Player discovers nearby Sacred Spring.

Zherca builds Rain Shrine.

Rain production begins.

### Minute 5

Player produces Vitez.

A small enemy patrol attacks.

### Minute 7

Player discovers central forest spirit.

Player can fight it or avoid it.

### Minute 8

Player discovers second Sacred Spring and enemy territory.

### Minute 10+

Player builds army.

Enemy launches small raids.

Player attacks enemy village.

### Minute 15

Enemy Grod is destroyed.

Victory screen.

---

# 23. Victory and Defeat

## Victory

Destroy the enemy Grod.

## Defeat

Player Grod is destroyed and the player has no way to rebuild.

For the prototype, no scoring system is needed.

---

# 24. What Is Explicitly NOT in the First Playable

Do not build these yet:

- multiple playable factions
- campaign cinematics
- hero leveling
- inventory
- items
- complex spell system
- naval combat
- walls and gates
- advanced diplomacy
- day/night gameplay
- weather simulation
- procedural maps
- multiplayer matchmaking
- advanced formation controls
- large technology trees
- dozens of folklore creatures
- complex armor/damage relationships
- historical simulation
- seasons
- farming
- food resource

These can easily destroy the scope of the prototype.

---

# 25. Minimum Viable First Playable

The build is considered successful when a new player can:

1. Start the game.
2. Understand that Vietras generate Wind.
3. Understand that Zhercas generate Rain.
4. Construct a Khata.
5. Construct a War Hall.
6. Construct a Rain Shrine.
7. Train Streletz.
8. Train Vitez.
9. Move an army across the map.
10. Fight enemy units.
11. Destroy the enemy Grod.
12. Reach a victory screen.

Everything beyond that is optional.

---

# 26. Most Important Test

The first playable is primarily testing one question:

> **Does watching ritualists generate Wind and Rain feel like a satisfying RTS economy?**

The second question is:

> **Does the combination of early-2000s RTS visuals and Slavic ritual fantasy feel distinctive enough that players immediately remember the game?**

If those two things work, the game has a foundation.

If they do not work, additional units, mythology, narrative, and graphical polish will not solve the fundamental problem.

---

# 27. Next Milestone After First Playable

Only after this prototype works should development add:

**Baba**

active rituals

additional supernatural creatures

day/night cycle

weather effects

second map

proper faction identity

technology upgrades

more sophisticated enemy AI

story missions

The first major expansion of the design should be **supernatural interaction**, not simply adding more conventional RTS units.
